# Step 2: swatch analysis

Generated by `research/tools/analyze.mjs` from the pinned SOTD Index. Every number below comes from the JSON files beside this one; do not edit by hand.

Two evidence types, kept apart:

- **Exact colors** (1308 sites, 2023-01-01 onward): real HEX values chosen by submitters. Lightness, chroma, hue and temperature are measurable. Ground is not.
- **Category swatches** (4491 sites, 2009–2022): one of ten fixed swatches per color. Only category-level statements are possible.

- `no-colors`: No colors recorded.
- `exact-before-cutover`: Exact colors recorded before 2023 (mostly 2022, the transition year). Excluded from the exact dataset because black-and-white records of the same period cannot be told apart from legacy swatches, so including only the chromatic ones would bias the set.
- `legacy-after-cutover`: A legacy hue swatch on an entry dated 2023 or later.
- `mixed`: Exact and legacy hue colors in one record.

## B. Category swatches, 2009–2022

Each recorded color is one of ten fixed swatches chosen by the submitter. A swatch names a color family; it says nothing about the site's actual lightness, chroma or exact hue, so Light/Dark (beyond black and white), Vibrant/Muted, Pastel, contrast and the 30° hue-family rule cannot be measured here.

Can establish: How many color categories a submitter recorded; Whether any hue category was recorded at all, or only black, white and gray; Which hue categories were recorded; Whether black, white or both were recorded; Whether a recorded hue is named warm (red, orange, pink, yellow, brown) or cool (blue, teal).

Cannot establish: Ground or figure; Lightness of a hue category; Vibrant versus Muted, or Pastel; Contrast; Monochromatic versus Two-Tone: red, orange and pink swatches sit within 18° of each other in OKLCH, so different swatches do not mean different hue families, and one swatch can cover several actual hues; Multicolor: no area shares are recorded.
